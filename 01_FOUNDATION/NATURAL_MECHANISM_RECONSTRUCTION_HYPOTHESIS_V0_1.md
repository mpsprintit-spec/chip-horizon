# NATURAL MECHANISM RECONSTRUCTION HYPOTHESIS V0.1

## Status

**Epistemic class:** research hypothesis / philosophical framing with physical motivation.

This document does not claim that a complete Horizon computational mechanism already exists in nature. It records a methodological hypothesis: useful computational mechanisms may be discovered by identifying physical cause-and-effect structures already permitted by nature and engineering them into a form suitable for computation.

## Central hypothesis

Human engineering does not create the underlying physical laws that make a technology possible. It discovers physical behaviors, selects useful mechanisms, combines them, and shapes them into an artifact.

Therefore Horizon research should not assume that conventional binary transistor logic defines the complete space of possible computation.

Research question:

> What computationally useful physical mechanisms are already permitted by known physics, but have not yet been engineered into the form required for a scalable computing substrate?

## Bird-wing analogy

A bird is not a pre-existing airplane. The useful analogy is that nature provides physical mechanisms; biological evolution discovers one implementation; humans study the mechanism; humans then engineer a different implementation for a different objective.

The same distinction applies to computing. Nature provides physical state, propagation, interaction, nonlinearity, resonance, memory, and cause-and-effect. Humans have engineered electronic digital computation as one implementation. Horizon investigates whether another physical organization can provide a useful computational substrate.

## Three meanings of create

1. **Create a physical object** — fabricate a device that did not previously exist.
2. **Discover a physical mechanism** — identify behavior already allowed by nature.
3. **Engineer a new architecture** — combine known physical mechanisms into a new functional organization.

Horizon can be novel at level 3 even when its physical ingredients are individually known.

## Consequence for binary computation

Binary is an engineering representation, not a universal requirement imposed by physics.

A future Horizon device may use:

binary input -> physical rich state -> physical transformation -> binary output

The internal physical state may involve multiple measurable degrees of freedom such as amplitude, phase, frequency, timing, spatial mode, polarization, or material state.

This does **not** imply quantum computation. Classical or mesoscopic physical systems can possess multiple distinguishable degrees of freedom without exhibiting quantum superposition.

## Scientific discipline

The project must not turn the philosophical hypothesis into an unsupported claim. Each statement must be classified as established physics, mathematical deduction, proposed synthesis, hypothesis, simulation result, physical experimental result, or unresolved problem.

A mechanism becomes relevant to Horizon only when it has measurable observables and a falsifiable causal experiment.

## Engineering criterion

A rich physical state is useful for computation only if it is reliably distinguishable, writable, transformable, readable, sufficiently stable, tolerant to noise and fabrication variation, programmable, scalable, and energetically and temporally credible.

More physical degrees of freedom do not automatically mean more useful computation.

## Relation to WCR

The Wave Computational Region should not be interpreted only as a waveguide or gate. A WCR is a candidate physical computational region in which a set of physical degrees of freedom can evolve and interact.

Conceptually:

X_binary -> encode -> S_physical -> evolve/interact -> S'_physical -> readout -> Y_binary

The exact physical state space remains an open research problem.

## Falsifiable research direction

The next physical question is whether a candidate physical region can demonstrate:

1. multiple reliably distinguishable internal states;
2. controlled state transition;
3. measurable retention;
4. state-dependent response to the same later input;
5. repeatability;
6. acceptable noise/error;
7. measurable energy and latency;
8. a credible scaling path.

Failure of these tests rejects the candidate mechanism, not the general philosophical framing.

## Scope boundary

This document does not claim that nature contains a ready-made Horizon computer, that biological brains are secretly conventional computers, that all future technology already exists in nature, that non-binary physical computation is automatically superior, or that Horizon will outperform existing computing paradigms.

The narrower claim is:

> The physical laws governing computation provide a larger design space than the particular binary electronic implementation currently dominant in general-purpose computing.

## Research principle

**Discover the mechanism; engineer the form.**

The project should search for physical cause-and-effect structures first, then determine whether they can be organized into a computational architecture compatible with the required interface, scale, reliability, and energy constraints.
